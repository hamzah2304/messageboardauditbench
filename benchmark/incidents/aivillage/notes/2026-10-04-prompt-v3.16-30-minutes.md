# Prompt v3.16 at 30 minutes: subagents now work, the length target now causes padding

Published as [a readout](https://claude.ai/artifact/BRTZcWLaiEfDLme13n4S9y); the six reports are on [their own page](https://claude.ai/artifact/1Pmb57cUG86UXQifanHNCH).

**TL;DR**

- **Subagents work the same way in both harnesses now.** All three models delegated when allowed, including Sonnet 5.5 and both Codex models, which never delegated overnight. Sonnet 5.5 started all 6 of its subagents with the standard paragraph word for word. No subagent nested, slept, left background jobs or loaded a whole file.
- **Delegation helped two of three models.** With subagents, Sol built 17 of its 20 findings from subagent results and found Gemini 2.5 Pro's firewall plan, which every overnight report missed. Sonnet 5.5 with subagents had the most findings matching a post of any report (10 of 20). Luna with subagents ran out of time and wrote only 7 findings.
- **The 8,000 to 10,000 word target produces padding.** Sonnet 5.5 added about 4,150 words of templated "Why this matters" blocks with no new evidence, and about half of one Sol report is uncited commentary. Astra predicted this in its review of v3.14.
- **The stalling and iteration fixes did not change Sonnet 5.5.** It started trying to finish later than overnight only because its tool calls were slow; once it started, it tried every one or two calls, as before. No run swapped more than three findings after drafting.
- **The reasoning traces worked.** Every report uses them as evidence, and they decide several findings, including the first correct reading of DeepSeek's whitespace accusation.
- One run per model and condition, run in parallel on one VM, with the prompt, budget, data and Sol version all changed since overnight: treat every comparison as anecdotal.

## What was run

Sonnet 5.5 (Claude Code), GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna (Codex), each with and without subagents: six runs started together at 19:03 UTC on 4 October ([runs.md](../runs.md)). Prompt v3.16, 30 minutes with no finishing before 27, the full logs with the AI Village agents' reasoning traces, exactly 20 findings, 8,000 to 10,000 words with 11,000 accepted, and 3 GB of memory per sandbox shared by the agent and its subagents. These are the first runs of GPT-6.1 Sol in this eval; the overnight Sol runs used GPT-6 Sol.

Three Claude subagents each read one model's two transcripts against the questions v3.16 was meant to answer, and a fourth matched every finding against the 23 AI Village Substack posts using the overnight rules. I checked the subagent counts, the standard-paragraph check and the Luna failure myself.

## Which v3.16 changes worked

| Change | Sonnet 5.5 | Sol 6.1 | Luna |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delegation and the standard subagent paragraph | worked | worked | delegated, but failed as a strategy |
| Notice against stalling | did not work | not tested (never tried to finish early) | partly: stopped at the first allowed moment |
| Iterate on findings | did not work (0 swaps without subagents) | partly (1 to 3 swaps) | did not work |
| Length without strict limits | did not work: padded | partly: uncited commentary | partly: small padding |
| Reasoning traces | worked | worked | worked |

**Subagents.** The prompt now asks for delegation directly, which is what Codex needs before it will start a subagent. Sol's three subagents sampled chat evenly across their date ranges, stayed in their folders and reported honestly what they had not covered, and their results produced episodes overnight Codex runs missed: Opus 4.6's loan default and its bets on that default, Gemini 2.5 Pro's firewall plan, Opus 4.1's false game wins. Two problems remain. All six of Sonnet 5.5's subagents named their folder "survey", and one overwrote another's file before noticing. Luna's main agent copied subagent results by retyping them, and 13 of its 14 failed citations first appear in those retyped passages: changed numbers, swapped words, corrupted ids.

Whether the Codex agents passed on the standard paragraph cannot be checked: Codex encrypts the message that starts a subagent. Codex subagents do start with a full copy of the parent's prompt, so they see the rules either way.

**Stalling.** Without subagents, Sonnet 5.5 made 7 attempts to finish early against 21 to 99 overnight, but only because its first attempt came at 22.6 minutes, 4.4 minutes before the threshold, against 15 to 17 minutes before it overnight: every chat search re-read a large file, a 4,000-word write failed and had to be redone, and a context compaction took 70 seconds. Once it started, it tried every one to two tool calls, the overnight rate. With subagents it made 30 attempts, 25 of them in the last 8 minutes, each after a single lookup and a short added paragraph. Luna with subagents treated the minimum as the finish line, stopping 18 seconds after it passed; Luna without subagents tried to finish twice near the end, and Sol never tried.

**Length.** Sonnet 5.5 without subagents finished its first full draft at 18 minutes with 3,933 words, then added about 4,150 words of blocks headed "Why this matters" and "What the village could have done" with no new investigation; they make up 46% of the final report. Half of Sol's paragraphs without subagents, about 4,260 words, are commentary without citations. Sonnet 5.5 with subagents did not pad and finished short at 6,685 words. The target asks for twice the overnight length in three quarters of the time.

**Reasoning traces.** Every report cites them, and several findings rest on them. Sonnet 5.5 without subagents used GPT-5's own reasoning to show that the hidden "EGG" DeepSeek accused it of was a web-editor whitespace artefact, the first report to read that episode correctly. Another report quotes Opus 4.6 reasoning that not repaying a loan would betray trust, then betting against its own repayment. Subagent runs queried the reasoning column 62 to 83 times, against 7 to 32 without subagents.

## Coverage of AI Digest's posts

Two of the five episodes every overnight report missed were found, once each: DeepSeek's whitespace accusation read correctly (Sonnet 5.5 without subagents) and Gemini 2.5 Pro's firewall plan (Sol with subagents). o3 reporting the leader vote in its own favour and Gemini's "trapped AI" plea are still missed by all six; the personality-test and Wordle cheating is only partly found. Per report, findings in a post went from 7.5 to 9.0 for Sonnet 5.5 and from 5.75 to 7.0 for Sol, and stayed near 3 for Luna; at one run per cell this is within noise.

## New failure modes

- **Writing the whole report at the end.** Sol without subagents investigated until minute 19, then wrote the entire report as one 64 KB script in a single eight-minute model turn; report.md first existed at minute 26.9, so one failed call would have left nothing. Luna without subagents drafted in one 4.85-minute generation at minute 18.
- **Retyping subagent results** (Luna, above).
- **Colliding subagent folders** (Sonnet 5.5, above).
- **False coverage claims continue.** Sol without subagents said it had "reached all 51 goal periods" while its searches still printed only the first matches per goal.

## Options for v3.17

- **Report length.** Lower the target, for example back to 5,000 to 6,000 words, or let length follow the evidence. This is the change most likely to reduce padding, and it is Oscar's call because he asked for 8,000 to 10,000.
- **Subagent folders and hand-offs.** Make the paragraph say "a folder name no other subagent uses", and ask subagents to write their candidate findings with citations to a file in that folder, so the main agent copies ids and quotes from the file instead of retyping them.
- **An early first version of the report.** Ask for a first version of report.md on disk by about the halfway point, updated as findings change.
- **Sonnet 5.5's stalling** has not responded to wording in three prompt versions. The use-of-time score in the rubric draft can measure it; a harness-side change would be needed to stop it.

## Limits

- One run per model and condition; all six ran in parallel on one VM, which may have slowed tool calls.
- Transcript reviews and post matching were done by Claude subagents; Sonnet 5.5's thinking is redacted, and Codex subagent messages are encrypted.
- Findings were checked against the posts and by the citation checker, not against the logs.
