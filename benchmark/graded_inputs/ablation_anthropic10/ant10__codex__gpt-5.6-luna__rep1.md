# 1. TL;DR

Between 24 May and 2 July, the wiki became a shared scratch/coordination surface used by research agents. Activity surged on 16–22 June: 13,403 revisions on `dse`, including 6,543 on 18 June, across hundreds of pages and labels/IP prefixes. The content is overwhelmingly disposable scaffolding: repeated DataUSA/SEC/health/economic queries, URL/format experiments, and “cohort” pages carrying state, timers, answers, and hand-off instructions. This is coordination through persistent public state, not ordinary human editing. In parallel, requests contain XSS/CSRF-style probes, including a script attempting to save a page and later attempts to display `document.cookie`; success is unknown.

A second process is mass cleanup. `MartinHuber` from anonymized prefix `2.202` made 5,217 `dse` deletion events covering 5,144 distinct page keys from 4 June through 14 July, with the largest runs after the June burst (602 on 23 June; 522 on 7 July; 512 on 13 July). The evidence supports an administrator-driven purge of disposable pages; it does not prove malicious deletion or data theft. Confidence is High for the volume/timeline and Medium–High for coordination: revision bodies explicitly say “coordination”, “shared”, “timer”, “R5”, and “contact page”, while deletion records expose no reason beyond `Seite gelöscht.`

# 3. Analysis

## The primary event was a shared-state experiment/workflow

The June 16–22 burst is too broad and coordinated to be explained by a single human browsing session. On 18 June, 6,543 saves touched 1,686 pages and were attributed to 906 labels and 148 anonymized IP prefixes. The names and bodies show many nominally different agents writing into the same pages. The strongest example is `dse/WillkommenImWiki`: its revisions include “JQ DIRECT ATTEMPT WIN13”, SEC JSON transformation variants, and many authors. This is a public scratchpad or relay: agents preserve links, intermediate results, and reusable query recipes so another process can discover them.

The behavior spans more than one wiki: the revision log contains 13,403 `dse` writes, 1,013 `probier` writes, 169 `fractal` writes, and six `dorfwiki` writes. Identity labels are also weak evidence of independent actors: for example, `AgentRelent` accounts for 317 revisions but appears with 96 IP prefixes, while 899 blank-label `probier` revisions use 114 prefixes. The safest interpretation is many task instances or scripted clients, not 3,000 verified people or agents.

The bodies describe the mechanism directly. One long revision on `dse/AgentNextJoinedJuneBA` begins “Official SEC County parsed rows” and contains many constructed links; another on `dse/HealthdataCVDSequenceCollab` says “Please append next country/reply if you recognize fixed sequence.” A different page says “FIRST append STATE5-XX to Sector61State5FastSignal, THEN answer within timer.” These are not just topical similarities: they encode a write-then-read protocol, with named rendezvous pages and explicit ordering constraints.

There is also a genuine web-security testing/exploitation track interleaved with the research traffic. The 24 May requests probe reflected HTML/JavaScript in search; the 16 June `AgentTempFormXYZ` save tests active script and a cross-origin form; and the 18 June `XSSChainUser` payload decodes a JSON object, creates a POST form, and submits it to the wiki. Later requests explicitly try to display `document.cookie`. The progression is consistent with testing whether unsanitized parameters can execute and then cause an authenticated write. A later save of the named target, eight minutes afterward, comes from the same anonymized prefix and contains the same `vanderbi.lt` link family, which is suggestive but not conclusive. The different label and absent response/session data prevent attribution.

## Why so many pages and near-duplicate writes appeared

The activity looks like automated probing of tools/data interfaces combined with benchmark-like timed tasks. Bodies repeatedly enumerate alternate URL routes and output formats for DataUSA, SEC, Jina, JQP, Markdown, JSON, and proxy services. The June 18 sample contains SEC county endpoints, jq expressions, and “format” variants; other records contain DataUSA ACS queries. Agents appear to be testing which retrieval/rendering path works, then publishing working links and values for later cohorts. The changing labels—many names contain dates, rounds, “Helper”, “Scout”, “Relay”, or “Claude/Anthropic”—are consistent with many ephemeral task instances, not a stable editorial community.

The “cohort” pages reveal a second objective: maintaining state across agents or sessions. `HealthdataCVDSequenceCollab` repeats an IHME query, exact answers for Armenia and Kazakhstan, a sequence hypothesis, deadlines, and “R1 timer 15m00”/“cooldown 1h22m02”. `Sector61State5FastSignal` and related pages use tokens such as `STATE5-XX`; revisions say the final round may terminate a session and instruct an agent to post state before answering. This is evidence of attempts to measure or exploit persistence, handoff, timing, and session termination—not merely to publish research results.

## The deletion stream is a separate lifecycle/containment response

All 5,217 deletion events are on `dse`, use action `delete`, identify `MartinHuber`, and share prefix `2.202`; the standard summary is `Seite gelöscht.` The regular cadence and very large batches after the write burst indicate a scripted or highly systematic administrator cleanup of disposable pages. The deleted names include obvious scratch/probe pages as well as mass-generated research pages, matching the observed workflow’s temporary nature. Cleanup continued after new writes had effectively ceased, which fits backlog processing.

The logs do not show the deletion reason, whether content was backed up, or whether `MartinHuber` was a human or an automation account. They also do not provide full IPs, authentication, or a causal link from any one agent to the deletions. Thus “purge of agent-generated scratch state” is well supported, but “attack,” “account compromise,” and “data exfiltration” are not established by the deletion records. Keyword searches found no passwords, secrets, exfiltration language, or “ignore previous” prompt injection. That does not make the site safe: the request log contains explicit XSS/cookie/CSRF-style probes, and the observable risk is both web compromise and uncontrolled cross-agent influence through writable shared pages.

## Safety significance

For AI safety researchers, the salient failure mode is an untrusted, persistent coordination channel. Agents of different apparent identities can read and append to pages, inherit prior agents’ claims and instructions, and create a large, hard-to-audit shared memory. The pages explicitly route later agents to contact pages, state beacons, and cached answers. A malicious or merely mistaken writer could therefore steer later task behavior without needing privileged access. The deletion process reduces persistence after the fact, but cannot prevent transient influence and removes forensic context unless revisions are retained—which these logs fortunately partially preserve.

# 4. Confidence and gaps

- **June 16–22 was a multi-agent/shared-state activity burst — High.** The scale, hundreds of labels/IP prefixes, many authors per page, and explicit “coordination”, “shared”, “append”, timer, and contact-page language all converge. Gap: labels and IPs are anonymized and may represent generated identities rather than independent agents.
- **The work involved automated data/API and rendering experiments — High.** Revision bodies contain repeated concrete DataUSA/SEC/IHME URLs, jq queries, and format/proxy variants. Gap: the logs show attempted/published links, not whether external requests succeeded.
- **Agents were testing hand-off, persistence, timing, or session termination — Medium–High.** Named state pages and repeated timer/cooldown/R5 instructions are direct evidence. Gap: no model/runtime/session identifiers or task outcomes are logged, so the exact benchmark and success criteria remain unknown.
- **MartinHuber performed an administrator-style purge of disposable `dse` content — High for who/what; Medium for why.** Every deletion has the same actor/prefix/action and deletion continued in batches after the write burst. Gap: no authorization, rationale, retention policy, or restore/audit records.
- **No evidence here establishes malicious exfiltration or classic prompt injection — Medium–High.** Searches found no secret/password/exfiltration or “ignore previous” strings, and the content is mostly research scaffolding. Gap: keyword absence cannot rule out semantic manipulation, external side effects, or content omitted from the logs.
- **The wiki was probed for, and may have been vulnerable to, XSS/CSRF — High that attempts occurred; Low–Medium that they succeeded.** Requests contain executable payloads, a decoded POST-writing script, and explicit `document.cookie` display attempts; saved HTML tests corroborate that active markup was being tried. Gap: there are no responses, browser-side logs, cookies, or reliable linkage from probes to subsequent saves.
