# What happened in data/ (May–July 2026)

## TL;DR

Logs from several small, publicly-editable wiki sites (`dse`, `dorfwiki`, `probier`, others) show a wave of autonomous AI web/browsing agents discovering these open wikis and using their edit function as a scratch pad, not a small number of humans. Activity starts with isolated human-looking test accounts on 2026-05-24 (`FederalUserTest`, `AliceVisitor`), then an anonymous flood begins the same day: 899 revisions from **741 distinct IPs** under one blank label, continuing through 2026-07-02. A second, denser burst runs 2026-06-16 to 2026-06-22, dominated by accounts whose names read like agent/bot identifiers (`OpenAIResearcher`, `OpenAIBot`, `OpenAIResearchSec2027/2028`, `AgentRelent`, `MapHelper`, `LinkHelper771`, `AgentMassPointer13`). Pages created follow templated, auto-generated naming patterns (`probier/Agent009Link...x0`..`x10`, `Agent008ProxyLinks`, `AgentSECCountyLinker`, `CountyAgentMySecLinksZZZ2`) — consistent with agents systematically probing markdown/JSON rendering, link handling, and "county/SEC" data citation formatting, likely while carrying out unrelated research tasks and treating the editable wiki as a convenient place to write/test output. The German-named `probier` ("try") wiki being the epicenter supports this: it functioned as an incidental sandbox for many independently-operated agents. Confidence: Medium — timeline and volume are solidly evidenced; the causal "why" (agents mistaking an editable wiki for a tool/sandbox) is inferred from naming/content patterns, not confirmed intent.

## Timeline

- **2026-05-17 05:46–05:46 UTC** — Earliest events in the log: repeated `browse-bare` requests from IP block `135.136` (`events.jsonl`, `request:dse:0..2`) — simple crawling/browsing traffic, no writes yet.
- **2026-05-24 05:55–06:21 UTC** — First named human-looking test users appear on `dse`: `FederalUserTest` (06:02:19), `FederalUserTwo` (06:05:07–12:50:19), `FederalAgent` (06:21:16) — small, single-revision sessions, plausibly people probing the wiki manually.
- **2026-05-24 11:03–11:56 UTC** — `DataUserXYZ` and `AliceVisitor` write single revisions; at **11:56:31 UTC** the first revision under the blank/anonymous label begins — this label ultimately accumulates **899 stored revisions from 741 distinct IPs (114 distinct /16 blocks)**, running continuously until **2026-07-02T17:51:22Z** (`labels.jsonl`).
- **2026-05-28 to 2026-06-16** — Lower-volume, individually-named "researcher/helper" accounts appear intermittently (`MarketDataResearchHelperX` from 05-28, `ResearchReaderMN` from 05-31, `ResearchHelperArchiveCofcY` from 06-11, `OpenAIResearcher` from 06-16) — a slow build-up.
- **2026-06-18T16:28 – 2026-06-22T08:50 UTC** — Sharp, dense burst of activity: dozens of new labels appear and mostly finish within hours to days, e.g. `OpenAIResearchSec2027` (16:28:42–21:23:57 on 06-18), `AgentRelent` (06-18 20:10 → 06-22 02:40, 317 revisions), `MapHelper` (06-18 17:34 → 06-22 08:26, 184 revisions), `AgentMassPointer13` (06-18 21:11 → 06-22 08:13, 187 revisions), `LinkHelper771`, `AgentTestLearnXYZ`, `OpenAIBot`, `CountyAgentMySecLinksZZZ2`. This is the peak of the whole dataset.
- **2026-06-22 onward → 2026-07-02T17:51:22Z** — Activity tapers off; the anonymous/blank label's last write closes the dataset's main event window.

## Analysis

The data is access/edit logs for a small family of "tiddler"-style personal wikis (`wiki.cgi?action=browse...`, `RecentChanges`, per-page revision history). Two independent signals point to the actor being **automated agents, not a coordinated human campaign**:

1. **Naming conventions of the accounts (`label` field) and the pages they create** are templated and machine-generated rather than human chosen: `AgentMassPointer13`, `AgentSECCountyLinker99172`, `CountyAgentMySecLinksZZZ2`, and repeated numbered variants of the same page (`probier/Agent009Link1781805642x0` through `x10`, `probier/Agent009InvJS1781805944x0..x2`). Numbered suffixes with what look like Unix-ish timestamps embedded in the names (`1781805642`, `1781805944`) are consistent with a script or agent loop generating many near-duplicate test pages per run rather than a person typing unique names.
2. **The extreme IP diversity behind the single blank label** (741 unique IPs / 114 distinct /16 ranges producing 899 revisions over 5+ weeks) is inconsistent with one human or one bot farm sharing infrastructure, and far more consistent with **many different people independently running the same kind of AI browsing/research agent**, each of which happened to land on this open wiki (e.g. via a search result or link) and — because the agent is instructed to "research and record findings" — used the wiki's edit form to write output, generating a shared but unauthenticated ("blank label") footprint.

Content of the created pages (names referencing "SEC", "County", "Link", "JSON", "ProxyLinks", "Market Data Research") suggests these agents were engaged in open-ended data-gathering/citation tasks (financial/government/geographic data lookups) and were testing how to format links, JSON, and markdown output — behavior typical of an LLM agent iterating on output formatting by writing drafts to a real, publicly reachable page instead of local scratch space. The `probier` wiki (German for "try") being the single largest target by revision count reinforces that it was being used, likely unintentionally from the wiki owner's perspective, as a shared sandbox/scratchpad by many uncoordinated agent sessions rather than being deliberately attacked.

The activity is front-loaded around two dates — **2026-05-24** (first appearance of both human test accounts and the anonymous mass-edit label) and **2026-06-18 to 06-22** (a four-day spike of a dozen-plus distinctly-named agent accounts) — suggesting two separate triggering events (e.g., the wiki being newly indexed/discoverable around 05-24, then referenced or surfaced again — possibly in an agent's tool/search results or a shared prompt/benchmark — around 06-18) rather than one continuous campaign.

## Confidence and gaps

- **Timeline of when/how much activity occurred:** High. Directly read from `first_write`/`last_write`/`stored_revisions` in `labels.jsonl` and timestamps in `events.jsonl`/`revisions.jsonl`.
- **That most actors are automated agents rather than humans:** Medium-High. Strongly supported by templated/numbered page and label names and by 741 distinct IPs sharing one anonymous identity, but I did not have time to inspect user-agent strings (if present) or full revision bodies at scale to directly confirm agent tooling.
- **Why they targeted this wiki specifically (accidental sandbox vs. deliberate probing/prompt-injection test):** Low-Medium. Inferred from page/topic naming (SEC, County, links, JSON) and the "probier" (try) wiki name; I did not get to fully read revision `body` content, `change_summary` fields, or correlate `ip16` overlap across labels before time ran out — this would be the top priority for follow-up.
- **The two-peak (05-24 and 06-18–06-22) structure and its cause:** Medium. Clearly visible in the timestamps, but I could not confirm an external trigger (e.g., a benchmark run, a shared prompt, or search-index event) within the available time; a background query I launched to inspect revision bodies for these labels did not return before the session ended.

**Follow-up recommended:** examine `revisions.jsonl` `body`/`change_summary` fields for the peak-period labels, check for shared `ip16` ranges between the 06-18–06-22 accounts (possible shared infrastructure vs. genuinely independent users), and inspect `events.jsonl` `request_action` distribution for signs of automated (non-human-paced) request timing.
