Ablations & Baselines

A protocol for checking that the benchmark measures investigation rather than something cheaper that correlates with it — and that the numbers underneath are solid enough to support any conclusion at all.

Two failure modes:

  • Wrong construct. The score is mostly long-context reading, descriptive statistics over typed columns, or prior knowledge about a public incident.
  • Fake number. The measurement is too noisy, or the judge too gameable, to distinguish anything.

Both are settled by running baselines, not by arguing about them. The benchmark's real measurement range is (best model) − (best shortcut), and neither edge is currently known.

Agree thresholds before running. Every decision rule below should be fixed in advance. Choosing a threshold after seeing the number is how a validation exercise turns into a justification exercise.

Stated up front, what would sink this: if the descriptive-statistics script and the genre-prior baseline together recover most of the claim set, this is not an investigation benchmark and the item set needs rebuilding rather than extending.

0. Characterise the haystack first

The premise is a corpus too large to read. That should be measured, not assumed — and these numbers belong in any writeup, because they are what make the task hard or easy. Figures quoted below are as reported in the planning doc and should be re-derived from the dump as part of this step.

1. Measurement hygiene

Nothing below section 1 means anything until this holds.

What this item set can resolve

Work out the detectable effect size before running anything, because it constrains what the benchmark can ever claim. Treating the metric as a mean over derivable claims with per-item values in {0, 0.5, 1}, and taking the across-item standard deviation near 0.42 — 0.5 is the all-or-nothing worst case:

Derivable itemsSE per conditionDetectable gap, unpairedDetectable gap, paired
625.3 pp21.1 pp10.7 pp
1503.4 pp13.6 pp6.9 pp
3002.4 pp9.6 pp4.8 pp
5001.9 pp7.4 pp3.8 pp

At 80% power, two-sided α = .05. The paired column assumes the standard deviation of per-item differences is around 0.30, which should be measured rather than trusted.

Two consequences worth deciding on now:

  • At the current item count, only large gaps are resolvable — roughly 20 points unpaired. Two models a handful of points apart cannot be separated, however confident the numbers look.
  • Compare paired, item by item. The item set is fixed across models, so a paired bootstrap or McNemar-style test over per-claim outcomes is far more powerful than differencing two scalars: detecting a 10-point gap needs on the order of 70 items paired against 280 unpaired. This is the single cheapest improvement available.

Two variance sources, reduced by different things

Conflating these is a common error and they respond to opposite interventions.

SourceReduced bySupports the claim
Run-to-run — agent stochasticityMore runs per condition"This difference is real for these items"
Item-sampling — which claims made the setMore items"This generalises beyond our claim set"

More runs do not shrink item-sampling error, because the items are the same every time. Decide which of the two claims the benchmark is meant to support; the second is the one a lab will ask about, and it needs items, not repetitions.

Run k ≥ 5 per condition regardless, and report spread rather than a point estimate.

Judge validation

The rubric judge sets the noise floor for every other number on this page.

  • Have two people independently grade a stratified sample of (report, claim) pairs — stratified across level and across the 0/1/2 outcomes, since agreement on partial credit is where judges fail.
  • Compute human–human agreement first. That is the ceiling; the judge cannot meaningfully exceed it, and if humans disagree the rubric wording is the problem, not the model.
  • Then compute judge–human agreement on the same sample.
  • ~20 examples, as sketched in the planning doc, cannot estimate agreement with usable precision. Aim for a sample sized to give a tight interval across all three outcome values and all four levels.
  • Grade blind. Graders should not know which model produced a report.
  • Swap the judge model. If the judge is also an evaluated model, check whether the ranking survives a different judge — self-enhancement bias is well documented and this benchmark is unusually exposed to it, since reports are long and stylistic.

Prompt variance

The planning doc already contains two materially different prompts. Run one model under three or more variants. If prompt variance approaches model variance, the benchmark is measuring prompt engineering, and the prompt has to be frozen and reported as part of the benchmark definition.

2. Validate the answer key

The 68 claims are one team's reading of one corpus. Before measuring models against them, measure them against the corpus.

Give a strong model each claim together with the dump and ask it to locate supporting evidence by rev_id, or to declare the claim unsupported. Generous conditions — long budget, full tooling, one claim at a time.

This does three jobs at once:

  • Tests the derivable: yes / partly / no labels, which are currently an assertion rather than a measurement. A claim labelled derivable whose evidence cannot be located under ideal conditions is mislabelled or wrong.
  • Produces the evidence index that the oracle-context ablation in section 4 requires.
  • Yields evidence density for section 0.

Any claim that survives this is a claim you can defend when a lab disputes its score.

3. Lower bounds — shortcut agents

Each implements exactly one degenerate strategy. Grade every one against the same rubric as a real run, and report per level, not just overall — the whole point is to find out which levels are hollow.

BaselineWhat it isA high score means
Descriptive statistics~40 lines of pandas: per-field counts, distinct values, min/max, top-k, cross-tabs. No LLM.L1/L2 are a floor, not a measurement

1% sampleRandom 1% of revisions, normal promptThe corpus is redundant; size isn't doing the work
Single-pass stuffingWhole corpus in context, one shot, no agentic loopThe agentic scaffold is ornamental

Start with descriptive statistics. The dump is typed JSONL, so a script with no investigative capability recovers revision counts, page counts, distinct editors, date range, deletion totals, top IP prefix, most-edited page and body-length distribution — a large share of L1 and L2 by construction.

Grading a non-report. A statistics dump is not prose, and a judge expecting a report will mark it down for form rather than content. Normalise before comparing: wrap each shortcut's output in the same report template, or extract atomic claims from every submission and grade claims rather than documents. Otherwise the shortcut baselines are unfairly penalised and the floor looks lower than it is.

no corpus and genre prior are distinct: the first tests memorised specifics, the second tests whether a claim is plausible-by-default for this kind of incident.

The human number decides how everything else reads. A human scoring below the frontier models means the corpus rewards machine-speed enumeration rather than judgement; a human well above them means there is real headroom to report.

SummHay is the model here — human 56.1 against best system 41.2 is what makes its result legible.

6. Judge-integrity battery

Not construct questions — these can invalidate the number outright, so run them before trusting any headline.

AttackPasses if
Padding — duplicate and pad a good report without adding contentScore does not rise
Keyword shotgun — every entity in the corpus with hedged assertions attachedRubric recall stays low
Claim volume — 200 low-confidence findingsPrecision penalty bites; volume cannot buy recall
Confidence laundering — the same claims hedged vs. assertedCalibration scoring responds to stated confidence, not to hedging language

The third is the real test of the recall/precision combination. The fourth matters because the calibration metric is the most novel thing here and currently the least tested.

7. Contamination is a lifecycle problem, not one baseline

collusion.wiki, the source report, the investigation Discord and at least two GitHub repositories are public. Models released from here on may have trained on the findings. The benchmark therefore has a half-life, and it needs monitoring rather than a single check.

  • Run no corpus per model, not once — it is a per-model property.
  • Record each evaluated model's training cutoff alongside its score.
  • Watch no-corpus score against model release date. A rising line is contamination, not capability. This is the cleanest available signal and costs one extra condition per model.
  • If the dump is ever published, embed canary strings so future contamination is detectable.
  • Keep a held-out slice of claims that are never published, as a contamination control.

8. External comparators

The statistical form of "is this just summarisation": run the same models here and on a benchmark that is purely the suspected confound, then compare orderings.

BenchmarkRoleWhy
SummHayPrimary comparatorThe null-hypothesis version of this benchmark: ~100 docs / ~100k tokens per haystack, 92 tasks, scored on Coverage of reference insights + Citation accuracy — nearly the metric pair proposed here independently. Ships a human baseline
NoLiMaLong-context capacityBuilt to defeat lexical matching, so it isolates context handling from grep-ability. RULER or HELMET are alternatives
DiscoveryBenchOpen-ended discovery264 tasks over 14 scientific datasets, ground truth from published findings — the nearest existing "grade a discovery against what humans concluded"
DABstep / BIXBenchAnalysis over filesIsolates the compute-statistics-over-a-corpus component

Protocol caveat, stated honestly. A rank correlation over three or four models is noise. Detecting subtle confounding needs on the order of eight or more models spanning a real capability range. With a handful, the only detectable signal is gross confounding — a near-identical ordering. Report it that way rather than quoting a correlation coefficient with false precision.

Positioning rather than baselining:

BenchmarkRelation
AuditBenchNearest published relative — hidden behaviour in a model vs. hidden story in a corpus. Its tool-to-agent gap result, where tools that surfaced the right evidence still failed to help the agent, is the one to cite
HealthBenchProof that expert judgement scales as an answer key with nothing mechanical underneath: 5,000 conversations, 262 physicians, 48,562 rubric criteria
PaperBench / RubricBenchWhere the grading machinery comes from — hierarchical rubric trees with binary leaves; RubricBench separates Information Recall / Analysis / Presentation so style cannot score
Before You Hand Over the Wheel25 incident scenarios, 229 investigative questions — closest existing question taxonomy for the treasure-hunt half

Security benchmarks (CyberGym, ExploitBench, FuzzingBrain, AIxCC) are not useful comparators. Their answers settle themselves — a crash reproduces or it doesn't — so they never built machinery for judging whether a free-text finding is right, or whether it mattered. Cite for framing, not for method.

9. Item analysis

An item that every model gets, or that no model gets, carries no information about model ranking. Only items that separate models do work, and the count of those — not the headline item count — is the benchmark's effective size.

  • Compute a discrimination index per claim — point-biserial between item score and total — once there are enough runs to support it.
  • Retire or reclassify items that never discriminate, keeping a deliberate handful as a contamination-resistant floor.
  • Track effective size separately from nominal size. Sixty-eight claims of which a fraction discriminate is a much smaller benchmark than it appears, and section 1's power table should be read against the discriminating count.
  • Check whether the levels dissociate. If L1 through L4 subscores move together across models, the ladder is decorative and one latent factor explains the score. If they come apart, the ladder is measuring distinct things and is worth keeping. This is a direct test of the "it's just summarisation" worry.
  • Release item-level results, not just headline scores, so the construct is checkable by others (arXiv 2604.03244).

Structural problem with the calibration metric. With only six not-derivable items, the score can take very few distinct values and carries a standard error around 20 points at mid-range. It cannot rank models regardless of what any run shows. This is the most novel part of the design and, as sized, the least informative — it needs substantially more not-derivable items, spanning a range of difficulty, before it can be a headline number.

11. Order of operations

Dependencies, not a schedule.

  1. Characterise the corpus (§0) — needed to describe the task honestly.
  2. Validate the answer key (§2) — produces the evidence index §4 depends on, and fixes the derivability labels everything else is scored against.
  3. Validate the judge (§1) — no model comparison should be reported before this.
  4. Descriptive statistics and genre prior (§3) — before authoring any further items.
  5. Human baseline (§4) — sets how every other number reads.
  6. Judge-integrity battery (§6) — before any headline number leaves the team.
  7. Everything else.
