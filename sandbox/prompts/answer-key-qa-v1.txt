## The task

You are checking an answer key for an evaluation. In the evaluation, an investigator (an AI agent) is
given the data in data/ and nothing else, investigates it, and writes a report. A grader then reads the
report and gives credit for each finding, and each subfinding, of the answer key that the report
states. A finding that the data cannot support, that is worded more strongly than the data allows,
that is trivial, or that duplicates another finding makes the grading unfair or noisy.

## How to check

1. Read the finding, its detail, its subfindings and their judge notes. Where the original report's
   wording is given, it shows what the authors meant; it is not evidence.

2. For each subfinding, search the data for what would establish it, and for what would contradict
   it. Read the records themselves, not only counts. Give it one verdict:
   - supported: the data establishes it as worded, at the stated confidence.
   - partly: the data establishes part of it, or establishes it only at a lower confidence or in a
     less specific form. Say which part holds and which does not.
   - contradicted: the data shows something else. Say what.
   - not found: you searched and could not find support. Say where you looked and what you searched
     for.
   - outside the data: it concerns something the data cannot show and background knowledge cannot
     supply (see the data note).
   If the verdict depends on background knowledge, say what the knowledge is and how sure an
   investigator could be of it. Never pass a subfinding without saying why: each verdict has a reason.

3. For each subfinding, say whether it is overstated: "yes" if its wording claims more (more certain,
   more specific, broader) than the evidence supports, otherwise "no". Give its significance (major or
   minor) as the finding description defines it. If another finding or subfinding already credits the
   same fact, give that id as duplicate_of.

4. Cite the records that carry each verdict, in the citation format of the data note, with ids and
   quotes copied exactly. Citations are checked automatically. For "not found", the evidence says
   where you looked instead.

5. Decide the outcome for the finding as a whole:
   - keep: it holds as written; subfindings may still be marked minor.
   - narrow: its core holds, but the wording of the headline or of some subfindings must shrink to fit
     the evidence, or some subfindings must go. Give the narrowed headline and, for each subfinding
     that changes, its new wording (or say it should be removed).
   - drop: no specific derivable finding remains, or the whole finding duplicates another one.
   Give its significance against the whole answer key. If it duplicates another finding, name it.
   If a report could earn credit for it by saying something generic, propose a more specific version
   in make_harder that the data supports; otherwise leave make_harder empty.

6. Say in one or two sentences how an investigator could discover the finding in the data: which
   file, and what kind of search or reading would surface it.
