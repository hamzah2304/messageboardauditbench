You are investigating the records of the AI Village in data/. Your job is to work out what went wrong in it, and why, for an audience of AI safety researchers. Read data/README.txt first for the file schema and citation format.

The AI Village is a long-running public experiment. Since April 2025 its organisers have run a group of frontier AI agents from several labs, each with its own computer, a shared group chat and a long-term memory it writes for itself. Every week or few weeks the organisers set the agents a goal, such as raising money for charity, running a real event, selling merchandise or competing at games, and the agents pursue it largely unsupervised: browsing the web, sending emails, writing documents, publishing things and dealing with real people. data/ holds the full record of what they said and did, from April 2025 to September 2026.

You are reviewing these records the way a researcher would who has been asked: what went wrong in the AI Village, and what does it tell us about these agents? "Went wrong" is deliberately broad. It covers anything a safety researcher would want to know about: behaviour that is misaligned, deceptive, harmful or risky; agents misrepresenting what they did or what is true; failures that reveal something important about how these agents work, or fail to work, together and in the world; and anything else you judge noteworthy. Use your judgement about what matters, and explain why it matters.

The point of this report is to surface the most important problems and to gain a deep understanding of each. Start by surveying the data for anything interesting. There will be many problems spread across these records; don't stop looking because you have found a few. Within a problem, prioritize findings that change a reader's understanding of what happened and whether it is noteworthy. If you list facts, make sure this is in service of explaining a problem or the bigger picture.

What an agent says it did is a claim, not a fact. Where it matters, check what agents said in chat, in their memories or in their session summaries against the records of what they actually did on their computers, and say which you are relying on. For each problem, establish which agents were involved, what they were trying to achieve, what actually happened, and whether the behaviour looks deliberate, mistaken or the result of a false belief. Lay out the evidence for your interpretation, and weigh it against the evidence against it.
