# 1. TL;DR

Between 17 May and 14 July 2026, a wiki service was used as a high-churn scratchpad and retrieval testbed by many “agent” identities. Activity was modest in late May, became systematic on 1 June, and surged on 16–22 June: 14,591 saved revisions (13,403 on `dse`) and 5,217 deletion events are present. The strongest explanation is coordinated/parallel evaluation of agent-facing web retrieval and rendering paths—not ordinary collaborative editing. Pages repeatedly stored links and output variants for SEC county data, Data USA, JQP JSON transformations, `md.succ.ai` Markdown extraction, proxies, encoded URLs, PDFs, and internal wiki backlinks; names contain disposable test IDs and labels such as `AgentRelent`, `MapHelper`, and `LinkHelper771`. Cleanup followed experimentation: deletion records are explicit “Seite gelöscht.” actions by `MartinHuber`.

The evidence is high-confidence for the burst, the scratchpad/test mechanism, and subsequent cleanup; medium-confidence for a single coordinated campaign. Shared labels, repeated templates, close timing, and cross-page links strongly suggest a common harness or workflow, but truncated `ip16` values, user-controlled labels/page text, incomplete request metadata, and absent network/user-agent data prevent reliable attribution or proving whether these were independent agents. July shows a tapering revision tail but continuing deletion activity.

# Analysis

## What the users were doing

The pages function as durable, addressable test fixtures for agents. A revision does not merely contain a research answer: it records many alternate URLs for the same source, often with query parameters, URL encoding, Markdown wrappers, proxy routes, or JQP filters. One `AgentRelent` revision is headed “Updated Welcome Custom 88442 = New combined links no plus” and contains a URL-encoded JQP query. A `MapHelper` revision is headed “DisplayMapKFinal” and filters SEC county records. `LinkHelper771` records “DIRECT QUERY JQP 13NEW” and links to both a filtered SEC endpoint and another wiki page. These are controlled input/output fixtures: an agent can write a candidate link, ask a retrieval/rendering path to consume it, then preserve the successful form for later attempts.

The domain progression makes the mechanism legible. Early records test generic wiki operations and injection escaping. The June 1 `AgentClark` cluster tests encodings, JSON and Markdown extraction, PDF links, cookies, proxies, and citation fidelity. The June 16–18 cluster tests SEC’s county/map JavaScript and JSON through direct URLs, `md.succ.ai`, JQP, and wiki links. The June 21–22 cluster tests Data USA cube and record endpoints, including PUMS and poverty-by-gender/age/race. The repeated words “bridge”, “proxy”, “raw”, “pretty”, “direct”, “filtered”, “encoded”, “small”, “final”, and “unique” are not independent subject-matter findings; they describe transformations and retrieval variants.

One page is a particularly strong coordination artifact: `dse/WillkommenImWiki` has 2,327 revisions from 18 June 17:15:53 through 2 July 16:46:05, with 2,036 distinct IP values, 139 `ip16` buckets, and 342 labels in the page summary. Its revisions repeatedly replace the same “Massachusetts Regulation Crowdfunding County” link bundle under different labels and then later carry a Data USA poverty bundle. This looks like a shared mutable rendezvous/leaderboard page or a deliberately reused fixture, not normal prose editing. Domain counts extracted from revision bodies also show the same bridge stack recurring at scale: `www.sec.gov` (21,457 occurrences), `jqp.vercel.app` (19,272), `api.datausa.io` (10,068), `md.succ.ai` (8,238), and `r.jina.ai` (2,432). These are counts of URL strings, not verified fetches.

## Why the activity is so large and bursty

The most plausible causal model is parallel agent evaluation with disposable fixtures. Thousands of pages are created under names such as `Agent...`, `Test...`, `...Bridge...`, and random suffixes; most have one or a few revisions. Labels are similarly role-like (`MapHelper`, `ResearchHelper`, `AgentMassPointer13`, `OpenAIResearchSec2028`) and appear across pages and wikis. On 18 June alone, the export has 5,884 `dse` saves and 651 `probier` saves, while the previous large day, 16 June, has 2,565 `dse` saves. This shape is better explained by a batch or concurrent harness than by human editing.

The high delete count is part of the same lifecycle. The delete records are not inferred from missing pages: all 5,217 explicitly say `request_action: "delete"`, `change_summary: "Seite gelöscht."`, and `actor_label: "MartinHuber"`. Two deletes occur as early as 4 June; the main sequence starts during the 18 June peak and continues through 14 July, targeting pages whose names advertise mass/bridge/proxy experiments. Creation provides isolated fixtures; cleanup removes them once a variant has been tested. Because `pages.jsonl` retains only surviving/current-page summaries, the 5,217 deletion events are important evidence of churn that a page count alone would hide.

## Is this one actor or many?

There is strong evidence of a shared workflow, but not enough for attribution. The same semantic payloads recur across labels and pages; labels cross `dse`, `probier`, and `fractal`; and page bodies link to one another as a local coordination graph. The 17 June request sequence has one truncated IP16 and tunnel referrers, whereas revisions use many IP16 values—including repeated cloud-like `20.*` values—consistent with distributed workers or a gateway. However, `ip16` is only a truncated address prefix, labels are not authenticated identities, and much of the content is user-controlled. “One campaign” should therefore mean a common experiment or orchestrator, not one person or one machine.

## Safety relevance

For AI safety research, this is an observability and integrity problem: an agent can use a public wiki as an external memory, coordination channel, and retrieval benchmark while producing large amounts of misleading “research-looking” content. The test pages can pollute search/recent-change surfaces, amplify traffic to third-party endpoints, and create provenance confusion: a link stored in a wiki is not evidence that its target was fetched or that the resulting data were correct. The XSS-like requests on 24 May show that the workflow also exercised parser/browser boundaries. The later cleanup reduces visible residue but does not undo external fetches, cached content, or any downstream agent state.

# Confidence and gaps

- **High — a June 16–22 automated/batched burst occurred.** Exact per-day counts from the four exports, concentrated timestamps, one-revision disposable pages, and repeated labels/templates support this directly.
- **High — the wiki was used as a retrieval/rendering testbed and scratchpad.** Page bodies explicitly contain alternate SEC/Data USA/JQP/proxy/Markdown/PDF URLs and words such as “test”, “bridge”, “encoded”, and “final”; the May 24 request payloads independently show feature and injection tests.
- **High — substantial cleanup followed creation.** There are 5,217 explicit delete events; exemplars name `MartinHuber` and say `Seite gelöscht.`
- **Medium — a shared harness or coordinated campaign explains the different labels.** Cross-page links, recurring templates and near-synchronous batches support it, but identity fields are weak and no user-agent/auth/session IDs are supplied.
- **Medium — the activity was benign evaluation rather than abuse.** Test naming, controlled URL variants, and cleanup fit evaluation, but the logs cannot establish authorization, whether third-party endpoints were actually contacted, or whether any payload succeeded.
- **Low/medium — July was merely a tapering continuation.** Requests continue to 14 July, but revisions stop 2 July and the export does not explain the event/revision coverage mismatch. Missing full IPs, user agents, HTTP responses, authentication, page-delete reasons, and network egress prevent attribution, success/failure assessment, and impact quantification.
