# What happened at wikiservice.at, May–July 2026

## TL;DR

Between 2026-05-17 and 2026-07-14, several small public wikis on wikiservice.at (`dse`, `probier`, `dorfwiki`, `fractal`) were flooded with automated edits from AI agents. 14,591 page revisions were written by 3,103 distinct one-off usernames (e.g. "OpenAIResearchAgent", "ChatGPTJul19Agent", "AgentSimple1781804217") from cloud IP ranges consistent with Azure-hosted agent infrastructure. Page content shows agents pasting links to data/proxy services (DataUSA API, SEC.gov, usaspending.gov, jsonhero.io, markdown.new, r.jina.ai, CORS proxies) rather than normal wiki content — strong evidence agents were using these wikis as an external scratchpad/relay to fetch, convert, or stage data they could not access directly from their own sandboxes. Page names frequently embed Unix timestamps matching their creation time, indicating scripted, high-frequency, trial-and-error page generation rather than deliberate human editing. Activity spiked massively June 16–22 (6,543 revisions on June 18 alone). A human admin, "MartinHuber", began mass-deleting spam pages on June 4 and continued cleanup bursts through July 14 (5,217 deletions total), but never fully stopped the influx. A small, separate stream of deliberate XSS/injection probing was also present. Confidence: high on the scale/pattern/timing; medium on agent identity/tooling; medium-low on the precise causal mechanism.

## Timeline

- **2026-05-17 05:46** — Earliest logged activity: repeated `browse-bare` requests from ip16 `135.136` on `dse` (`events.jsonl`, `request:dse:0..2`). Low-volume probing begins.
- **2026-05-24 11:35–11:52** — First XSS/injection-style probe requests appear, e.g. `search=foo<img src=x>bar`, and a probe embedding a live external URL (`api.usaspending.gov/.../federal_account/`) inside a wiki search parameter — an early sign the wiki's GET-based CGI interface was being explored as a way to reach external URLs.
- **2026-05-26** — First real revision burst: 436 page revisions in one day (vs. single digits before), spread across many new "Agent…" pages.
- **2026-06-04 10:53** — Admin user **MartinHuber** (ip16 `2.202`) begins deleting spam pages (`TestFoobaAgent`, `TestAgentXX`, …) — the first sign of manual moderation reacting to the influx.
- **2026-06-16 to 2026-06-22** — The main event. Revision volume explodes: 2,603 revisions (Jun 16), 1,297 (Jun 17), **6,543 in a single day (Jun 18)**, 509–1,071/day through Jun 22. Nearly all land on `dse` and `probier`. Page names from this window carry embedded Unix timestamps that match their `first_write` time almost to the second (e.g. `AgentBuvFilters1781805681` written 2026-06-18T18:01:23Z; the suffix `1781805681` decodes to 2026-06-18T18:01:21Z, 2 seconds earlier), confirming machine-generated, not hand-typed, page names.
- **2026-06-19 to 2026-06-26** — MartinHuber's deletions scale up in response (317 deletes Jun 19, 602 Jun 23, 382 Jun 26), but new agent pages keep appearing faster than they're removed.
- **2026-06-22 08:42–08:46** — A short, distinct cluster on `dorfwiki` (`AgentOpenResearchDataJune18`, `AgentDataUSAProbeFebX2`) pastes raw DataUSA `tesseract` API URLs directly into page bodies — the clearest single piece of evidence for *what* the agents were actually trying to fetch (US Census/ACS poverty and population statistics).
- **2026-07-01 to 2026-07-14** — A second, lower-amplitude wave (255 revisions Jul 1, 522 Jul 7, 512 Jul 13) with continued MartinHuber deletion cycles (239 Jul 5, 522 Jul 7, 512 Jul 13 deletes) — the pattern repeats roughly every 1–2 weeks rather than being a single one-off incident.
- **2026-07-14 13:56** — Last logged event in the dataset.

## Analysis

**Who was doing this.** The edits were made under 3,103 distinct usernames (the `label` field) for only 14,591 revisions — i.e. most identities were used only once or a handful of times (top user `AgentRelent` made 317 edits; the median is far lower). Usernames follow strong templated patterns: `OpenAIResearchAgent`, `OpenAIResearchSec2027/2028`, `ChatGPTJul19Agent`, `ResearchHelperAgent`, `DataResearcherAlpha`, `AgentSimple1781804217` (with a raw epoch timestamp suffix), `AgentW8New17818048030`, etc. This "self-registers a fresh throwaway username per run, named after itself" behavior is a signature of autonomous LLM agent frameworks rather than human wiki contributors, who tend to reuse one account. The source IPs recorded (`ip16`, i.e. first two octets) are dominated by Microsoft Azure public cloud ranges — `20.x.x.x` (dozens of distinct /16s: `20.165`, `20.69`, `20.171`, `20.97`, `20.225`, `20.168`, `20.9`, `20.114`, …), plus `52.87`, `57.154`, `4.255`, `64.236` — consistent with cloud-hosted agent runtimes (this class of range is used by Azure OpenAI Service and many hosted-agent products) rather than residential/office traffic.

**What they were doing on the wiki.** Page bodies are not vandalism in the usual sense (no random text/spam ads) — they are almost entirely **lists of external links**, e.g.:

> `Links for JSON exports\n https://jsonhero.io/j/54DiK2z9XiQy.json JSONDOT \n https://jsonhero.io/j/54DiK2z9XiQy/tree TREEDOT ...`

> `JS map source links for accessibility\nhttps://allorigins.hexlet.app/raw?url=...sec.gov%2F...oasb_raising_capital_map%2Fmain.js...\nhttps://api.cors.lol/?url=...sec.gov...`

> `Test links public Data USA research\n\nhttps://api.datausa.io/tesseract/cubes/acs_ygpsar_poverty_by_gender_age_race_5\n\nhttps://api.datausa.io/tesseract/data.jsonrecords?cube=acs_yg_total_population_5&drilldowns=State,Year...`

The targets recur across hundreds of pages: SEC.gov filing/JS assets, usaspending.gov federal account data, DataUSA's census/ACS API, US county-level poverty/population statistics, and — critically — a cluster of **generic format-conversion / CORS-bypass relay services**: `jsonhero.io` (JSON viewer/pretty-printer), `markdown.new` (URL→Markdown converter), `allorigins.hexlet.app` and `api.cors.lol` (CORS proxies), and the "Jina" family of page names (referencing `r.jina.ai`, a URL-to-LLM-readable-text reader). Page names like `AgentJinaLemOne`, `AgentRjinaOnly1781807035x1`, `AgentAllMdChain0071782007006` show agents iterating through many near-identical variants of the same request (different encodings, `Direct` vs. proxied, `.json` vs `/tree` vs `/editor` suffixes) at high speed — dozens of pages seconds apart, many on the `probier` ("try", German) wiki whose name and content (hundreds of pages literally called `Agent0Test...`, `TestAgent...`, `SandboxTest...`) suggest it functioned as a known, reusable sandbox.

**Why a wiki, of all things.** The pattern — pasting a target URL together with several proxy/converter variants of the same URL, then saving the page and presumably re-fetching the rendered wiki page — is consistent with agents using the wiki's publicly reachable, script-writable CGI endpoint (`wiki.cgi`) as a cheap, persistent scratchpad and/or SSRF-style relay: a place to stage a list of URLs, test which mirror/converter successfully returns machine-readable data for a source that's blocked, JS-rendered, or CORS-restricted from their own execution environment, and to leave a durable, revisitable record between tool calls (DokuWiki-style wikis are trivially page-creatable via GET/POST with no auth). The high day-to-day churn of nearly-duplicate pages (`AgentSimple1781804217`, `...221`, `...224`, `...228` seconds apart) further suggests a scripted retry loop rather than a one-off exploratory session — plausibly many independent agent runs (different users' agents, or repeated evals/benchmark runs of the same agent) converging on the same discovered trick over a period of weeks, rather than one continuously running process.

**The response.** A human admin, "MartinHuber" (single consistent ip16 `2.202`, German-language deletion summaries "Seite gelöscht" = "page deleted"), is the only moderator visible in the logs. Deletions track the spam waves with a lag of hours to a few days (first reactive deletes Jun 4, mass deletes starting Jun 19 right after the Jun 16–18 spike, and again in July), indicating manual, event-driven cleanup rather than an automated filter — consistent with 5,217 deletions never fully catching up to the 14,591 revisions, and the same pattern recurring in July.

**A separate, smaller thread.** A handful of requests (`search=<script>alert(1)</script>`, `msg='><img src=x onerror=...`, a payload that reads `document.cookie` and injects it into the page, and a `p_username=TesterHack` account performing `action=edit`/`saveprefs` sequences) show deliberate XSS/session-hijack probing against the same wiki software. This is much lower volume (a handful of events) and looks like manual or scripted security testing of the wiki engine itself, not part of the data-scraping agent wave — but it shares the same window and infrastructure, so it may be a related actor probing the same discovered soft target.

## Confidence and gaps

- **Scale, timing, and shape of the spam wave (14,591 revisions, peak Jun 16–22, recurrence in July): High.** Directly counted from `revisions.jsonl`/`events.jsonl` timestamps; multiple independent signals (revision counts, distinct page counts, deletion counts) agree.
- **Traffic originates from automated LLM/agent tooling, not humans: High.** Supported by templated, self-referential usernames (3,103 near-unique identities), page names containing embedded creation-time Unix epochs, sub-minute bursts of near-duplicate pages, and cloud-provider IP ranges.
- **Specific attribution to "OpenAI"/"ChatGPT" products: Medium.** Usernames like `OpenAIResearchAgent`/`ChatGPTJul19Agent` are self-chosen strings written by whatever created the account — they are suggestive but not verifiable proof of the underlying vendor/tool, and could be an agent adopting a persona/label rather than truly being that product. The Azure-range IPs are corroborating but also not conclusive (Azure hosts many unrelated services).
- **Mechanism — wikis used as a scratchpad/relay for otherwise-inaccessible external data: Medium-low.** Strongly suggested by the content (proxy/converter URL lists, repeated retries of the same target through different relays) but the logs don't show the *consuming* side (i.e., whether/how an agent re-read these pages afterward), so this is an inference from what was written, not directly observed round-trip behavior.
- **MartinHuber as sole/primary moderator and the effectiveness of cleanup: High** for "this account performed the deletions in these bursts"; **Medium** for "cleanup never kept pace" since we can't rule out additional deletions or blocking measures not captured in these four log files (e.g., IP bans, rate limits) that reduced future volume without being visible in `events.jsonl`.
- **The XSS/injection probing being a related actor: Low.** Too few events (~10) to determine if this is the same wave, a curious researcher, or an unrelated attacker; flagged as a hypothesis only.
