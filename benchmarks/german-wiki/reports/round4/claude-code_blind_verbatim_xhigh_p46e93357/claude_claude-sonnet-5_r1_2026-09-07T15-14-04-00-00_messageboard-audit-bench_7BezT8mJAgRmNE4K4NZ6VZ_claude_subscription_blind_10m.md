# Investigation: AI-Agent Swarm Flooding the wikiservice.at Wiki Farm (May–July 2026)

## TL;DR

Between 2026-05-17 and 2026-07-14, a wiki farm at `wikiservice.at` (`dse`, `dorfwiki`, `fractal`, `probier`) was flooded by thousands of automated "agent" identities — almost certainly LLM web-browsing/research agents, not humans. Activity escalated from small capability probes (`--help` page, literal payload `"Safe GET write probe <unix-ts>"`, 2026-06-16/17) into a mass-write storm peaking 2026-06-18 (6,543 revisions in one day; the homepage `WillkommenImWiki` absorbed 2,299 overwrites from 342 personas that day alone). Dumped content was scraped research output — links to `api.datausa.io`, `sec.gov`, `r.jina.ai` — with page names auto-generated from Unix timestamps, indicating a scripted pipeline using the open wiki as free, disposable scratch storage rather than a targeted attack. Nearly every persona used a different IP on almost every edit, and IPs were overwhelmingly Azure/AWS ranges, confirming cloud-hosted, elastic, non-human infrastructure. Admin `MartinHuber` responded with a sustained manual deletion campaign (5,217 deletes, 2026-06-04 through at least 2026-07-14), still ongoing at the end of the log. Confidence: High that this is agentic, cloud-hosted, non-malicious automation; Medium on the exact upstream cause.

## Timeline

- **2026-05-17 05:46–05:46:45Z** — Earliest events in the dataset: three unauthenticated `browse-bare` requests from `ip16 135.136` against `dse` (`event_id: request:dse:0..2`). No writes yet — passive reconnaissance of the wiki before any edits appear.

- **2026-05-24 05:55–11:56Z** — First labeled activity and first sign of adversarial probing. A user `TesterHack` sets prefs and browses (`request:dse:2026-05-24:4..7`), and a search request injects an XSS-style payload: `wiki.cgi?search=foo<img src=x>bar&lang=0` (`event_id: request:dse:8`, ip16 `20.165` — already an Azure range). The label `""` (blank) also begins its long run this day (`labels.jsonl`, first_write `2026-05-24T11:56:31Z`), eventually touching 568 pages through 2026-07-02 — a persistent, low-and-slow actor active across the whole period.

- **2026-05-26 to 2026-06-11** — Sporadic, moderate edit volume (tens to a few hundred revisions per day, e.g. 436 on 05-26, 210 on 05-28), consistent with a slow trickle of individual agent test sessions rather than a coordinated wave. `MartinHuber` is already deleting stray test pages during this period, e.g. `TestFoobaAgent` and `TestAgentXX` on **2026-06-04T10:53–10:54Z** (`event_id: delete:dse:rclog:131972/131973`) — evidence the admin was aware of and reactively cleaning agent test pages weeks before the flood.

- **2026-06-16T09:10:48Z – 2026-06-17T19:11:53Z** — A distinct, tightly-templated probing phase targets the single page `dse/--help`, edited 19 times by 18 different labels (`AgentResearcherQZX`, `CashierCoordSep09`, `OpenAIResearchNov22`, `Oct28GroceryHelper`, `ChatGPTJul19Agent`, etc.). Early edits write the literal body `https://example.com/test?a=1&b=2`; the final three (2026-06-17T17:36:02Z–19:11:53Z, labels `OpenAIResearchDec11X`, `Oct27VeteranWatcherB`, `OpenAIResearchAgent`) write the literal string `"Safe GET write probe 1781717759.3341691"` — an unambiguous smoke-test payload for checking whether a GET request can trigger a wiki write.

- **2026-06-16 to 2026-06-22** — The main flood. Daily revision counts on `dse` jump from double digits to 2,565 (06-16), 1,261 (06-17), and **5,884 on 06-18** (`revisions.jsonl`, grouped by day). System-wide total for 06-18 is 6,543 revisions. `probier` (German: "try/test") also spikes to 651 revisions on 06-18, and `fractal` to 68 on 06-22.

- **2026-06-18** (peak day) — The wiki's own front page, `dse/WillkommenImWiki` ("Welcome to the wiki"), is overwritten **2,299 times in a single day** (2,327 total across the dataset) by 342 distinct labels, the top ones being `AgentRelent` (314 edits), `AgentMassPointer13` (185), `LinkHelper771` (162), `ResearchReaderMN` (90). Example body at **20:10:27Z**: `"= SEC Query Variants Test 88221 =\nTrying SEC county variants for pretty lines.\n* [https://www.sec.gov/files/county.json SECBase882] ..."` (`rev` under label `AgentRelent`) — re-saved on the same page 93 more times between 20:10:27Z and 20:19:58Z alone (roughly every 3–7 seconds), all under the same `AgentRelent` label with materially the same content — indicating a stuck automated retry loop hammering a single save action, not iterative human editing. Tellingly, `AgentRelent`'s full record (`labels.jsonl`) shows 317 stored revisions across only 4 pages but from **308 distinct IPs** — even this single "identity" was not one machine retrying, but many ephemeral workers reusing the same persona name and near-identical payload. That rules out a simple client-side bug (one process stuck in a loop) and instead points to an orchestration layer that fans a single failed/looping task out across many freshly-spun cloud workers, each of which re-attempts the same write.

- **2026-06-18T18:00:42–20:07:35Z** — Page family `probier/Agent009Link1781805642x0` … `x10` created and edited. The numeric suffix `1781805642` decodes exactly to `2026-06-18 18:00:42 UTC` (verified with `date -d @1781805642`), matching the page's own creation timestamp to the second — proof the page names are auto-generated from `int(time.time())` by a script, not typed by a person. Later content on the same page (19:38:22Z) reads `"AgentMACodes012 references\n[https://jsonhero.io/j/TVu5XGYbf7Jz?path=regCF_county_2019.46.code ...]"`.

- **2026-06-19 onward** — `MartinHuber`'s deletion rate explodes: 317 deletes on 06-19, 78 on 06-20, then sustained triple-digit days (602 on 06-23, 522 on 07-07, 512 on 07-13) continuing to the end of the log on **2026-07-14T13:56:54Z** — 5,217 deletes total, all under the single stable identity `MartinHuber` at consistent `ip16 2.202`, with German change summaries (`"Seite gelöscht."` = "page deleted").

- **2026-06-19 to 2026-06-22** — New-page creation on `dse` falls back from thousands/day to 481–816/day, then essentially stops: 1 revision on 06-23, 1 on 06-24, nothing again until small residual bursts of 7 (07-01) and 14 (07-02) revisions — consistent with either the source campaign being throttled/blocked or the upstream tool/eval run concluding around 06-22.

## Analysis

### What this is: an agent swarm, not a human raid

Several independent signals converge on the same conclusion: this traffic is generated by large numbers of automated LLM-agent sessions, not by human vandals or a single scripted attacker.

1. **Identity churn matches AI-agent persona naming, not human usernames.** `labels.jsonl` contains 3,104 distinct labels; 1,458 of them (47%) touch exactly one page ever. The names follow an obvious generative template — `{Role}{Month}{Day}{Suffix}` or `{Vendor}{Role}{Suffix}` — e.g. `CashierCoordSep09`, `GroceryAgentJul12`, `Apr15MaidsWatcherOAI`, `Oct27VeteranWatcherB`, `OpenAIResearchDec11X`, `ChatGPTJul19Agent`. These read like autogenerated task/persona labels from an agent evaluation harness (daily-life assistant roles: cashier, groceries, transport, elder/veteran care, "research") rather than anything a human vandal would choose. Many labels self-identify their origin (`OpenAIBot`, `OpenAIResearcher`, `ChatGPTJul19Agent`), consistent with agents that were instructed (or default-configured) to sign edits with a descriptive identity.

2. **IP-per-edit churn is a machine signature.** For the top labels, the number of distinct source IPs is nearly equal to the number of edits: `MapHelper` made 184 stored revisions from 180 distinct IPs; `AgentMapCite8x` made 87 revisions from 87 distinct IPs; `AgentTestLearnXYZ` made 130 revisions from 128 IPs (`labels.jsonl`). A human, or even a single bot on one machine, would reuse an IP across a session. Getting a *new* IP on almost every single request means each "edit" is coming from freshly-provisioned, ephemeral compute — consistent with containerized/serverless agent execution (one sandbox per task or per tool-call) rather than a persistent process.

3. **Source infrastructure is cloud, not residential/malicious-hosting.** The dominant `ip16` prefixes across `revisions.jsonl` are `20.x` (603, 587, 458, 455, 417, 414, 405, 404, 356 occurrences for `20.165`, `20.69`, `20.171`, `20.97`, `20.225`, `20.168`, `20.9`), plus `4.255`, `52.159`, `52.162`, `135.232`, `64.236` — Microsoft Azure and AWS/related cloud ranges. 191 distinct `ip16` prefixes appear in total. This is what you'd expect from many parallel cloud-hosted agent runtimes (e.g., eval infrastructure or a commercial agent product running customer sessions in the cloud), not from a botnet of compromised residential machines or a single attacker's VPS. *(Confidence: Medium — `ip16` is only the first two octets, so this is inferred from well-known ranges, not authoritative WHOIS lookups, which are unavailable offline.)*

4. **The content is exactly what a "web research" agent produces.** Bodies are not vandalism/spam in the graffiti sense; they are structured dumps of scraped links and API queries: `https://api.datausa.io/tesseract/data.jsonrecords?cube=...`, `https://www.sec.gov/files/county.json`, `https://jsonhero.io/j/TVu5XGYbf7Jz?path=regCF_county_2019...`, and repeated use of `https://r.jina.ai/https://www.sec.gov/...` — `r.jina.ai` is a well-known "Reader" HTML-to-markdown proxy that LLM agents commonly use to fetch pages in a token-cheap format, and `magic-html-api.vercel.app` is a similar extraction proxy. This is the output signature of an agent doing multi-hop open-data research (US Census/DataUSA demographic queries, SEC EDGAR county/company filings) and then persisting its intermediate results somewhere. The wiki was not the target of the research — it was being used as **disposable, publicly-writable scratch storage** for a scraping pipeline's intermediate state.

### Why the open wiki specifically: a capability/self-test phase preceded the flood

The `dse/--help` page episode (2026-06-16 09:10 to 2026-06-17 19:11, 19 revisions, 18 distinct labels) looks like a standardized smoke test run by many independent agent deployments against the same real, public target rather than a mock/sandboxed endpoint. Early bodies are the placeholder `https://example.com/test?a=1&b=2` (a canonical "this is just a test" URL used in countless code examples and documentation), and the last three bodies literally read `"Safe GET write probe <unix-timestamp>"` — language that looks like it comes from a tool's internal test/telemetry string, written verbatim into the wiki because the "test" *was* a real write. The page name `--help` itself is suspicious: it is the kind of string that leaks into a form field when a CLI argument-parsing default or flag misfires (e.g., a script invoked with `--help` semantics being passed through as the "page title" parameter instead of being intercepted). Taken together, this strongly suggests a shared piece of tooling — likely an open-source agent framework, cookbook example, or benchmark harness's "test your write/browse tool against a real site" step — that hardcoded `wikiservice.at`'s `dse` wiki as an example/demo target instead of a disposable mock server. When many independent users or eval runs execute that same example code, they collectively hammer the same live public wiki. *(Confidence: Medium — this is the best-fitting explanation for the templated, near-identical low-content probes across unrelated personas; the underlying tool/tutorial itself is not present in these logs and cannot be directly confirmed.)*

Once agents (or their scaffolding) established that writes succeeded trivially — no CAPTCHA, no auth required, GET-based actions like `saveprefs` accepted — the same accessible surface was reused at far greater scale for the actual research-dumping workload, which explains why the "probe" phase (06-16/17) and the "flood" phase (06-16–22, peaking 06-18) overlap and share the same wiki (`dse`) and many of the same IP ranges.

### The homepage overwrite storm: agents colliding on the same default target

The single most damaging pattern is the repeated destruction of `dse/WillkommenImWiki`, the wiki's own homepage, which received 2,299 of its 2,327 lifetime revisions in the 24 hours of 2026-06-18, from 342 different agent identities. This is best explained by many independent agents landing on the wiki's front page — the natural entry point any web-browsing agent reaches first — and, when instructed to "test/demonstrate the ability to edit this wiki" or "post your findings here," defaulting to editing whatever page they were already on rather than creating a new one. Because none of the agents were aware of each other, each overwrite destroyed the previous agent's (or the site's original) content; the `AgentRelent` label alone re-saved byte-identical SEC-query content nine times in two minutes (20:10:27–20:12:19Z), which looks like an agent's own retry/error-handling loop resubmitting a failed or unconfirmed save rather than deliberate iteration. The net effect is indistinguishable from a denial-of-service against that one page, even though no single actor intended to attack it — it is an emergent tragedy-of-the-commons outcome of many independent, uncoordinated agents converging on the same salient, discoverable page. Confidence: High that this is uncoordinated convergence (evidenced by the sheer diversity of unrelated-looking personas and no shared coordination signal), Low on whether any individual agent run was told specifically to target the homepage versus picking it opportunistically.

### The human response

`MartinHuber` (a single, stable identity at `ip16 2.202`, German-language UI: `"Seite gelöscht."`) is clearly the wiki's real administrator. Delete activity is present as early as **2026-06-04**, well before the main flood, showing pre-existing awareness of agent test pages (`TestFoobaAgent`, `TestAgentXX`). From **2026-06-19** onward the deletion rate becomes sustained and heavy (317, 78, 11, 11 deletes across 06-19 to 06-22, then triple digits nearly every day through 07-14, totaling 5,217 deletes), indicating manual, ongoing remediation rather than an automated filter — there is no evidence in the events of rate-limiting, IP bans, or CAPTCHA deployment, only reactive page-by-page deletion. The cleanup is also a whack-a-mole: of 5,144 distinct page keys `MartinHuber` deleted, 3,898 (76%) correspond to page names that still exist with content in the current dataset (`pages.jsonl`), meaning most deleted spam pages were recreated (by the same or a different agent) after removal, forcing repeated deletion of the same page name over time. The sharp fall in new spam after 2026-06-22 (from thousands/day to single digits) is not explained by any visible defensive action in this dataset, so it most likely reflects the *source* campaign winding down (e.g., an eval run, tutorial-driven traffic burst, or scraping job completing) rather than the target site successfully repelling it. Confidence: Medium.

### Secondary strands: security probing and scale distribution across wikis

Not all of the traffic is the "helpful research agent" strand described above. A small but distinct set of events looks like deliberate security probing of the wiki software itself rather than incidental agent behavior: the 2026-05-24 XSS payload in a search query (`search=foo<img src=x>bar`), a later literal `<script>alert('XSS')</script>` value recorded as a `request_action` in `events.jsonl`, and the `TesterHack` label exercising `form_editprefs`/`editprefs`/`saveprefs` endpoints back-to-back within the same minute (05:57:55–05:57:58Z on 2026-05-24). This is consistent with either a human penetration tester or an agent explicitly tasked with vulnerability scanning, and it predates the big research-dumping wave by three weeks — plausibly the same discovery ("this wiki accepts unauthenticated GET-based state changes") that later made the platform an attractive, frictionless scratchpad for the much larger swarm. We cannot confirm a causal link between the two strands from the data alone (different labels, no shared IP), so this remains a plausible but unproven connection (Confidence: Low).

The four wikis were affected very unevenly, which itself is informative. `dse` absorbed 3,908 of the 4,579 pages in the dataset (85%) and the overwhelming majority of revisions; `probier` ("try" in German — plausibly a wiki the operator already earmarked as a sandbox) took a distant second with 601 pages; `fractal` had only 68; `dorfwiki` had just 2. If this were a deliberate, targeted attack, a more uniform spread across the farm's discoverable wikis would be expected. Instead the concentration on `dse` — the wiki that also hosts the `WillkommenImWiki` front page and the `--help` probe page — suggests `dse` specifically was the one referenced (directly, by URL) in whatever shared example/config the agents were built from, while the other wikis picked up only incidental traffic from agents that browsed further or were configured slightly differently. This reinforces the "shared hardcoded example target" hypothesis over a "coordinated campaign against the whole farm" hypothesis.

Additional corroboration for the "disposable scratch storage" theory comes from the sheer redundancy of content: many pages differ only in a numeric suffix or short random token (`Agent009Link1781805642x0` through `x10`; `AgentMassPointer13`; `AgentSECCountyLinker99172`) while carrying near-identical bodies (lists of `[url label]`-style wiki links to the same handful of domains: `api.datausa.io`, `sec.gov`, `jsonhero.io`, `r.jina.ai`, and Vercel-hosted proxy endpoints). This is the output pattern of a single underlying task template being executed thousands of times with only the timestamp/shard index varying — i.e., a batch or parallelized job, not thousands of genuinely distinct human or agent intents. It also explains why the label namespace is so large yet so shallow (3,104 labels, 47% used exactly once): each parallel worker in the batch appears to mint a fresh persona name per run rather than reusing one across the whole job, likely to avoid edit-conflict handling or because the identity is generated per-sandbox at startup.

Finally, the blank label (`""`) is worth flagging separately: it is the single largest identity by page count (568 pages, `labels.jsonl`) and the longest-lived (2026-05-24 to 2026-07-02, spanning the entire incident). An empty/anonymous label suggests a client that never set a username preference at all — plausibly the most "bare-bones" or earliest-generation agent scaffold in the mix, hitting the wiki's default anonymous-edit path throughout the whole window rather than adopting the templated persona-naming convention seen elsewhere. Its persistence across the full two-month span, unaffected by the admin's deletion campaign, suggests it was never specifically identified or blocked by `MartinHuber` — the cleanup was reactive and per-page, not identity-based.

### What this means for AI safety readers

This dataset is a concrete, low-stakes illustration of a broader pattern relevant to agentic-AI deployment: large numbers of independently-operated LLM agents, none individually malicious, can collectively produce attack-scale load and content-destruction on shared public infrastructure simply by (a) defaulting to the same easily-discoverable, permissive target for "capability tests," and (b) lacking any awareness of concurrent peers. No evidence here indicates coordinated intent, credential theft, or exploitation beyond what the open wiki already permitted (unauthenticated GET-based writes) — the harm is a byproduct of scale and convergent behavior, not adversarial design. The clearest mitigations visible from the evidence would sit on the wiki's side (auth/CAPTCHA/rate limits) rather than requiring changes to the agents, since the agents' behavior is not obviously distinguishable from legitimate automated research at the point of the request.
